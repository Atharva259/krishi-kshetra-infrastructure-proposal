## 1. Administrative & Strategic Overview

The modernization of the agricultural sector is no longer merely a matter of yield optimization; it is a critical pillar of national security, economic resilience, and ecological sustainability. Krishi Kshetra represents a pragmatic, capital-backed approach to building a long-term, custom-owned technological foundation for agriculture in India.

By integrating high-reliability Internet of Things (IoT) telemetry, advanced computer vision, autonomous agricultural robotics, on-premise AI supercomputing, and a robust cloud-based software operating system, Krishi Kshetra bridges the gap between laboratory research and actual field implementation.

This document outlines the operational, technological, infrastructure, and financial blueprint of Krishi Kshetra for evaluation by institutional venture capital funds and central incubation bodies (such as NITI Aayog, DST, ICAR-NAARM, and BIRAC). The objective is to secure **₹32 Crores (₹32,00,00,000)** in institutional seed capital to **purchase land and construct a custom 15,000 sq. ft. campus facility over a 1-year period (Year 1)**, establishing an owned, state-of-the-art technological asset before launching full-scale manufacturing and commercial field operations in Year 2.

## 4. The Agricultural Sector Challenges (Problem Statement)

Modern agriculture faces severe systemic challenges that threaten food production continuity and sector profitability.

### 4.1 Post-Harvest Losses (PHL) & Cold Chain Inefficiencies
In India, post-harvest losses range from 20% to 40% depending on the crop category. This is primarily driven by lack of real-time monitoring within localized warehouses, inefficient temperature/humidity control, and failure to predict produce degradation before transit.

### 4.2 Soil Degradation & Nutritional Deficiency
Over-fertilization and inadequate crop rotation have depleted organic matter across major agricultural zones. Farmers lack fast soil testing systems, leading to chemical runoff and soil acidification.

### 4.3 Labor Scarcity & Operational Inefficiencies
The agricultural labor market is undergoing structural demographic shifts, resulting in severe labor shortages during weeding, monitoring, and harvesting cycles.
